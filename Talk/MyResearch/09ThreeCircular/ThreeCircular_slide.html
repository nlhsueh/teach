Bibliographic & Research Overview: This paper (Lin, Yang, & Hsueh; Fundamenta Informaticae, IOS Press, preprint dated 2008 / published 2009, pp. 1–15) presents a spatial-domain reversible data hiding (RDH) scheme based on three-circular-pixel (TCP) difference expansion. Core Innovation: In J. Tian's seminal 2003 Difference Expansion (DE) algorithm, an image is partitioned into non-overlapping 2-pixel pairs, capping single-layer capacity at 0.50 bits per pixel (bpp) and modifying up to four pixels to expand two differences. By grouping pixels into 3-pixel circular blocks and selecting the largest pixel as the shared central anchor, raising the central pixel expands two differences simultaneously—between the largest and smallest pixels, and between the largest and middle pixels. This increases the number of embeddable pixel pairs from N/2 to floor(2N/3) (up to 0.67 bpp per layer) while modifying at most three pixels instead of four, yielding lower mean square error (MSE) and higher PSNR across standard 512x512 benchmark images.

Structural Map of the Paper: - Section 1 (Introduction, pp. 2–3): Motivates lossless cover-image restoration in sensitive domains (such as medical diagnosis) and surveys four RDH paradigms: LSB compression (Fridrich et al.), generalized LSB quantization residual compression (Celik et al.), difference expansion (Tian and derivatives), and histogram shifting (Ni et al., Lin et al.). - Section 2 (Difference Expansion, p. 3): Reviews Tian's 2003 integer difference expansion and its overflow/location-map constraint. - Section 3 (The Proposed Scheme, pp. 3–11): Introduces TCP block transformation (3.1), formal embedding algorithm and central-pixel symmetry (3.2), blind extraction and threshold restriction 1 <= T <= 127 (3.3), and a worked 3x6 image walkthrough (3.4). - Section 4 (Experimental Results, pp. 11–14): Evaluates the Java implementation on Lena, Baboon, Airplane, and Boat against Tian's method, plus JBIG compression ratios across T = 1..127 (Table 1). - Section 5 (Conclusions, pp. 13–14): Summarizes the dual-difference expansion principle and lossless recovery mechanism.

Theoretical Context & Prior Art (Section 1): Reversible (lossless) data hiding requires exact bit-for-bit restoration of the original host image after secret message extraction. 1. Fridrich et al. [1, 2] compress the least significant bit (LSB) planes losslessly to create space; payload depends entirely on spatial compressibility. 2. Celik et al. [3] generalize this by quantizing each pixel x to Qx = q * floor(x/q), compressing the residual r = x mod q, converting compressed residual + payload into q-ary symbols s_i, and embedding via Qs = Qx + s_i (extracted via s_i = Qs mod q). 3. Ni et al. [12] shift pixel values between a histogram peak M and minimum point m by 1, then embed 0/1 at M. Because raw pixel histograms rarely have very high peaks, Lin et al. [13] improved this by shifting differences in non-overlapping 2-pixel blocks. 4. Tian [4] pioneered Difference Expansion (DE) in 2003, inspiring prediction-based variants such as Chang & Lu [6] (difference from average of preceding pixels) and Thodi & Rodriguez [7] (prediction-error expansion). The TCP scheme directly generalizes Tian's spatial pair expansion to 3-pixel circular blocks with a shared peak pixel.

Detailed Analysis of Tian's DE (Section 2): In Tian's DE [4], expanding the difference h = x - y between two pixels doubles it to h' = 2h, guaranteeing an even integer whose LSB is 0. Embedding a message bit b in {0, 1} sets x'' = x' + b. During extraction, the decoder recovers b = |x'' - y'| mod 2 and h = floor((x'' - y')/2), then restores: - x = x'' - b - floor(h/2) = 30 - y = y' + floor((h + 1)/2) = 33. Overflow & Location Map: For extreme pixel pairs such as (235, 250), expanding h = -15 would push y' beyond 255 (over-saturation). Thus, Tian requires a binary location map of size floor(N/2) bits indicating which pairs were expanded, losslessly compressed before embedding. Why TCP Improves on Both Axes: In the worked comparison (24, 28) and (28, 26), Tian expands them independently into (22, 30) and (29, 25)—modifying all 4 pixels with squared error (-2)^2 + 2^2 + 1^2 + (-1)^2 = 10. TCP treats (24, 28, 26) as one 3-pixel block sharing central peak 28, expanding it to (22, 30, 26)—leaving pixel 26 untouched with squared error (-2)^2 + 2^2 + 0^2 = 8.

TCP Block Definition & Dual Expansion Mechanics (Sections 3 & 3.1): A raw 3-pixel block [a_i, b_i, c_i] (scanned left-to-right, top-to-bottom) can take 1x3, 3x1, or L-shaped layouts (Fig. 1a). Arranging its three pixels on a directed circle (Fig. 1b) preserves cyclic adjacency while letting the largest pixel y_i act as the logical center, flanked by x_i (logically left) and z_i (logically right). Tie-Breaking Rule for Exact Reversibility: When y_i = x_i or y_i = z_i, the pixel visited earlier in scan order is uniquely designated as y_i (e.g., [26', 20, 26] maps to (26, 26', 20)). This deterministic canonicalization ensures both encoder and decoder construct identical TCP tuples. Worked Numerical Verification (Section 3.1): For (24, 28, 26), h_i1 = 4 >= h_i2 = 2. Expanding h_i1 symmetrically moves x_i down by floor(4/2)=2 to 22 and y_i up by floor(5/2)=2 to 30. Then z'_i = y'_i - 2*h_i2 = 30 - 4 = 26 remains completely unmodified! Embedding b_1 = 0, b_2 = 1 subtracts b_1 from x'_i and b_2 from z'_i to produce (22, 30, 25). At the receiver: h''_i1 = 30 - 22 = 8 (LSB 0), h''_i2 = 30 - 25 = 5 (LSB 1); original differences h_i1 = floor(8/2) = 4, h_i2 = floor(5/2) = 2; restored pixels x_i = 22 + 2 + 0 = 24, y_i = 30 - 2 = 28, z_i = 28 - 2 = 26. Note also the paper's additional MSE examples: (24, 28, 23) -> (23, 31, 21), (24, 28, 25) -> (22, 30, 24), and (24, 28, 24) -> (22, 30, 22)—all achieving lower MSE than two separate Tian pairs.

Formal Expandability & Changeability Theory (Section 3.2): For an 8-bit grayscale block [a_i, b_i, c_i] in [0, 255], expanding a difference and then subtracting a message bit b in {0, 1} must never cause underflow (< 0) or overflow (> 255). The `- 1` term in `x_i - floor(h_i1/2) - 1 >= 0` explicitly reserves headroom for embedding b_1 = 1 after expansion. Crucial Structural Property: Suppose h_i1 >= h_i2, which implies x_i <= z_i. If h_i1 is expandable, then y'_i <= 255 and x'_i - 1 >= 0. Because z'_i = y'_i - 2*h_i2 >= y'_i - 2*h_i1 = x'_i, we automatically have z'_i - 1 >= x'_i - 1 >= 0. Therefore, expandability of the larger difference guarantees expandability of the smaller difference in the same TCP block! Why Changeability Requires `(y_i - x_i) mod 2 = 1` or `x_i > 0`: To replace the LSB of a non-expanded difference h'_i1 without exceeding [0, 255]: if h'_i1 is odd, flipping its LSB to 0 adds 1 to x'_i (safe since x'_i < y'_i <= 255). If h'_i1 is even, flipping its LSB to 1 subtracts 1 from x'_i, which is only safe if x'_i > 0. Hence an unchangeable difference occurs solely when h'_i1 is even AND x'_i = 0.

Complete 8-Step Embedding Algorithm (Section 3.2): 1. Partition the N-pixel cover image into non-overlapping 3-pixel blocks and transform each block i into TCP form (x_i, y_i, z_i). 2. Classify each difference into EN (expandable with h <= T) or CN (changeable and not in EN), and expand pixels according to the four mutually exclusive cases (2.1 through 2.4). 3. Populate location map L of fixed size floor(2N/3) bits: e_{2i} = 1 if h_i1 in EN (else 0), e_{2i+1} = 1 if h_i2 in EN (else 0). 4. Compute post-expansion differences h'_i1 = y'_i - x'_i and h'_i2 = y'_i - z'_i. Collect the original LSBs of all changeable differences belonging to CN into list C. 5. Losslessly compress L and C together using JBIG to produce LC*. Because |L| = floor(2N/3) is known to the decoder from the image dimensions, only |LC*| (16 bits) and |C| (17 bits) need to be stored in the header. 6. Concatenate S = |LC*| || |C| || LC* || U. 7. Blindly replace the LSBs of all changeable differences (both expanded EN and non-expanded CN) using Equations (1) and (2). Notice that if h'_i1 is even (as for every expanded difference), x''_i = x'_i - b_i1 increases the difference by b_i1; if h'_i1 is odd (a CN difference), x''_i = x'_i + 1 - b_i1 leaves x'_i unchanged when b_i1 = 1 and increments x'_i by 1 when b_i1 = 0, setting the new difference's LSB directly to b_i1. 8. Inverse-rotate each stego TCP block (x''_i, y'_i, z''_i) back into its raw spatial order [a'_i, b'_i, c'_i].

Mathematical Properties & Edge-Case Rigor (Sections 3.2 & 3.3): 1. Central-Pixel Symmetry (Fig. 3, pp. 7–8): Given raw block [10, 12, 20], the largest-pixel TCP representation is (12, 20, 10) with h_i1 = 8, h_i2 = 10. Expanding gives h'_i1 = 16, h'_i2 = 20 and (x'_i, y'_i, z'_i) = (9, 25, 5), which maps back to [5, 9, 25]. If instead the smallest pixel (10) is chosen as the central anchor, the differences are h_i2 = 20 - 10 = 10 and h_i3 = 12 - 10 = 2. Because h_i2 = h_i1 + h_i3, doubling h_i2 = 20 = 2*h_i1 + 2*h_i3 yields h'_i3 = 4 and the exact same raw expanded block [5, 9, 25]. Thus, choosing the largest pixel is without loss of generality. 2. Proof of the 1 <= T <= 127 Constraint (Section 3.3, p. 10): If T = 0, then differences of h = 1 are NOT expanded (they stay in CN). When a block [29, 29, 30] has two differences of 1 and embeds bits "10", Eq. (2) adds 1 to z'_i = 29 (since h'_i2 = 1 is odd and b_2 = 0), turning z''_i into 30. Now a'_i = 30 ties with c'_i = 30, and because a'_i is visited before c'_i, the decoder's tie-breaking rule selects a'_i as y'_i instead of c'_i, rotating the TCP tuple to (30, 30, 29) and inverting the extracted bits to "01". Enforcing T >= 1 eliminates this ambiguity.

Lossless Decoding & Restoration Mechanics (Section 3.3, pp. 8–10): A major strength of the TCP scheme is that extraction of the raw bitstream S is completely blind to which differences were expanded vs. merely changeable: every expanded difference (EN) is guaranteed to be changeable in the stego-image. Once S is assembled, the decoder reads the fixed-width length headers |LC*| and |C|, extracts LC*, and invokes the JBIG decoder to recover: - L: the exact location map of length floor(2N/3) bits, distinguishing expanded pairs (e = 1) from non-expanded pairs (e = 0). - C: the exact original LSBs of all changeable differences in CN. In Step 6, the restoration is driven by the pair with the larger difference: - When both pairs were expanded (Case 6.1), the larger difference restores the largest pixel y_i and the smallest pixel (x_i or z_i) first; once y_i is known, the remaining middle pixel is recovered immediately by subtracting the halved smaller difference (z_i = y_i - h_i2 or x_i = y_i - h_i1). - For any non-expanded changeable difference (Cases 6.2–6.4), comparing the original control bit c with the extracted bit b tells whether the pixel was shifted by +1 (if c = 1, b = 0 -> subtract 1), -1 (if c = 0, b = 1 -> add 1), or unchanged (c = b). Thus, the host image is recovered with 0 bit error.

Step-by-Step Numerical Trace (Section 3.4, pp. 10–11): Figure 4 demonstrates the full lifecycle on a 3-row x 6-column image (18 pixels = 6 three-pixel blocks, 12 pixel pairs) with threshold T = 4: - Block 0: [62, 60, 59] -> TCP (59, 62, 60) -> expanded (58, 64, 60) -> embeds "10" -> stego (57, 64, 60) -> raw [64, 60, 57]. - Block 1: [60, 58, 61] -> TCP (58, 61, 60) -> expanded (57, 63, 61) -> embeds "00" -> stego (57, 63, 61) -> raw [61, 57, 63]. - Block 2: [64, 63, 60] -> TCP (60, 64, 63) -> expanded (58, 66, 64) -> embeds "11" -> stego (57, 66, 63) -> raw [66, 63, 57]. - Block 3: [61, 59, 20] -> TCP (20, 61, 59): h_31 = 41 > 4 (CN, e_6 = 0), h_32 = 2 <= 4 (EN, e_7 = 1) -> expanded (20, 62, 58) -> embeds "01" -> stego (20, 62, 57) -> raw [62, 57, 20]. Note that h'_31 = 62 - 20 = 42 (even, LSB = 0 saved in C). - Block 4: [60, 65, 57] -> TCP (60, 65, 57): h_41 = 5 > 4, h_42 = 8 > 4 (both CN, e_8 = e_9 = 0; LSBs 1, 0 saved in C) -> embeds "01" -> stego (61, 65, 56). - Block 5: [57, 59, 55] -> TCP (57, 59, 55) -> expanded (57, 61, 53) -> embeds "10" -> stego (56, 61, 53). All 12 bits of S = (100 011 0101 10)_2 are embedded, extracted blindly, and every pixel is restored bit-for-bit to Fig. 4(a).

Experimental Methodology (Section 4, pp. 11–12): To ensure a strictly controlled comparison, the authors implemented both the proposed TCP scheme and Tian's 2003 DE method in Java on the same Windows machine and evaluated them on four canonical 512x512 8-bit grayscale images spanning smooth to highly textured content: - Lena (smooth portraits + moderate edges) - Baboon (highly textured fur/whiskers—stress test for difference expansion) - Airplane (large smooth sky/fuselage regions) - Boat (mixed smooth water and fine mast details) Overhead Encoding Details: For N = 262,144 pixels, the location map L has a constant size of floor(2 * 262,144 / 3) = 174,762 bits, so |L| does not need to be transmitted. Only |LC*| (16 bits) and |C| (17 bits)—33 bits of fixed header—are prepended to LC* and the random user payload U. Both L and C are jointly compressed with JBIG before embedding.

Rate-Distortion Comparison Analysis (Section 4, Fig. 6, pp. 12–13): Figure 6 plots PSNR (dB) against embedding capacity (bpp) for single-layer embedding across all four 512x512 test images: - Lena (Fig. 6a): TCP stays strictly above Tian's curve from ~0.14 bpp up to 0.666 bpp (32.13 dB at T=127), whereas Tian's curve terminates below 0.50 bpp. - Baboon (Fig. 6b): Because Baboon has large pixel differences, PSNR drops more rapidly with capacity in both schemes; nevertheless, TCP maintains a clear PSNR advantage at equal capacity and extends total capacity beyond 0.65 bpp. - Airplane (Fig. 6c) & Boat (Fig. 6d): Both confirm consistent gains in both visual fidelity and maximum single-layer capacity. Quantitative Analysis of Table 1 & Edge-Case Discussion (Sections 3.4 & 4, pp. 11–14): Table 1 provides the exact empirical trade-off between threshold T, expandable space |E|, non-expanded changeable LSB count |C|, JBIG-compressed overhead |LC*|, and visual quality (PSNR) on 512x512 Lena (total changeable pairs |E| + |C| = 174,762): - At T = 1..7, |LC*| exceeds 174,762 bits (e.g., 299,336 bits at T = 1 and 189,000 bits at T = 7), meaning the JBIG-compressed overhead alone exceeds the total changeable capacity of the image. - At T = 8, |LC*| drops to 169,368 bits (< 174,762), leaving 5,394 bits (minus 33 header bits) for user payload U at 41.56 dB PSNR. - By T = 20, net user capacity surpasses 108k bits (~0.41 bpp net) at 37.07 dB; by T = 127, |LC*| shrinks to just 2,512 bits (69.6x compression ratio), yielding >172,000 net user bits (~0.66 bpp net) at 32.13 dB. Transparent MSE Boundary Analysis (p. 11): When h_i1 >> h_i2 (e.g., (40, 80, 76) with h_i1 = 40, h_i2 = 4), lifting y_i by +20 forces z'_i = 100 - 8 = 92 (a +16 shift on z_i), producing higher local MSE than Tian. The authors explicitly acknowledge this case and explain why natural image smoothness keeps it statistically negligible.

Summary of Conclusions (Section 5, pp. 13–14): The Three-Circular-Pixel (TCP) difference expansion scheme advances spatial-domain reversible data hiding through three coupled mechanisms: 1. Shared-Peak Dual Expansion: Increasing the largest pixel in a 3-pixel circular block expands two differences simultaneously, reducing pixel modifications per embedded bit pair and lifting single-layer capacity to 2/3 bpp. 2. Deterministic Reversibility: By enforcing 1 <= T <= 127 and restoring the larger difference first, the decoder recovers the largest and smallest pixels exactly and derives the third pixel from its halved difference without ambiguity. 3. Empirical Superiority: Across Lena, Baboon, Airplane, and Boat, TCP consistently surpasses Tian's 2003 DE baseline in both visual quality (PSNR) and embedding capacity.
