Key Takeaway: Programming Ability Index (PAI) normalizes a learner's exercise score by complexity-adjusted resilience r, rewarding high scores achieved with fewer setbacks. Talking Points: - Raw scores alone cannot tell a student who solved a problem cleanly from one who stumbled through ten failed attempts. - We define resilience r = exp(k * (tr - ch_p)), where tr counts setbacks (syntax errors and failed HTC submits) and ch_p is the problem's cyclomatic complexity. - As Figure 6 illustrates, dividing score s by r produces PAI: fewer setbacks yield r < 1 and a higher PAI. Across our course, mean PAI reached 121.80, indicating generally efficient problem solving. Transition: Next, let's see how we quantify testing ability using TQI and TEI.

Key Takeaway: Testing Efficiency Index (TEI) measures how much a learner's custom DTCs boost branch coverage and mutation killing over baseline OTCs, weighted by test-count efficiency. Talking Points: - First, Test Quality Index (TQI) takes the geometric mean of branch coverage and mutation score, and TQI+ computes the improvement ratio when adding learner DTCs to baseline OTCs. - To ensure writing DTCs is never penalized below doing nothing, learners who use only OTCs get TEI = 1, while DTC authors receive TQI+ * (1 + exp(-k * (te - ch_t))). - As shown in Figure 7, achieving strong coverage and mutant killing with concise, targeted test cases yields the highest TEI.
